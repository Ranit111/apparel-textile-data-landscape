# EEA Textile Indicator Analysis

Source: European Environment Agency, Circularity Metrics Lab.

## Key findings from the official indicator extract

- EU textile consumption was **19 kg/person/year** in 2022.
- Textile waste generation was **16 kg/person/year** in 2022.
- The household textile-waste capture rate was **15%** in 2022, meaning most household textile waste was not separately collected.
- **26%** of separately collected textile waste was sent to landfill or incineration in 2022.
- Primary raw-material use associated with EU textile consumption was estimated at **523 kg/person/year** in 2022.
- Supply-chain GHG emissions associated with textile consumption were estimated at **355 kg CO2e/person/year** in 2022.

## Derived indicators

The ratio of waste generation to textile consumption is approximately **84.2%** on a mass-per-person basis. This is a descriptive ratio only: the two EEA indicators have different methodological scopes and should not be interpreted as a material-balance equation.

The capture rate leaves an estimated **85%** of household textile waste outside separate collection. This identifies collection efficiency as a strong candidate KPI for future circularity analysis.

## Business interpretation

1. **Collection infrastructure:** the low capture rate suggests an opportunity to analyse collection performance by country when country-level data are available.
2. **Treatment capacity:** the landfill/incineration share indicates a potential downstream recycling-capacity bottleneck.
3. **Resource exposure:** the high primary-material-use figure supports monitoring virgin-material dependency alongside commercial KPIs.
4. **Sustainability analytics:** combining these indicators with trade and production data can identify where commercial growth and circularity pressure overlap.

## Limitations

These are official EEA indicators but they do not all share the same year, scope, unit or methodology. Some are modelled estimates. They must not be merged into one numeric index without explicit normalization and methodological review.
